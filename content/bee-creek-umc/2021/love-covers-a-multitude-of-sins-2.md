Opening with a personal story of festering hiking blisters that healed only once uncovered and dried, the sermon argues that sin must be honestly admitted, not ignored. It reframes sin not as a moralistic list of vices but, via the Greek hamartia ('missing the mark') and the priority of the First Commandment, as straying from relationship with God. The call is to admit our need and receive the grace that heals and covers sin, offering abundant life."

**Series:** 2021

> A fourth-week Easter sermon in Bee Creek UMC's 'Works of Love' series, preaching 1 Peter 4:8 ('love covers a multitude of sins') in light of 1 Corinthians 13. Opening with a personal story of festering hiking blisters that healed only once uncovered and dried, the sermon argues that sin must be honestly admitted, not ignored. It reframes sin not as a moralistic list of vices but, via the Greek hamartia ('missing the mark') and the priority of the First Commandment, as straying from relationship with God. Distinguishing justification from sanctification, it presents salvation as ongoing transformation toward holiness, and points to Jesus covering and overwhelming sin (Matthew the tax collector, the cross). The call is to admit our need and receive the grace that heals and covers sin, offering abundant life.

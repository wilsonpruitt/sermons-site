# Sermon Transcription — Builders and Names

The Bible is filled with a lot of ordinary names. Sure, there are kings and queens and prophets and emperors, but there are also a lot of shepherds, fishermen, and women who take care of the home. There are builders, not just leaders. 

A lot of cultures erase these ordinary names from their history. The chronicles of history are the playgrounds of kings and future kings. The only people worth remembering are the ones you would put on a coin. The Bible is different. The great cloud of witnesses includes both the ordinary and the extraordinary.

In Nehemiah 3 — we are still building the wall, but we have a bunch of names, and these names matter. Their story matters. There’s little attestation to these names beyond this chapter. For some of these names, it might be the only place in history where the name is written down.

Because of the rarity of ancient Hebrew as a language, one of the curious things of studying Hebrew is that the Hebrew Bible is the only extant document of ancient Hebrew. Sometimes there’s a word that appears once in Nehemiah and doesn’t appear anywhere else in all of human history. Usually, if you’re reading a book today or an article or the subtitle of a TikTok, and you come across a word you don’t know, you can use context clues. You can look it up. In English, we have this great tradition of the Oxford English Dictionary, which tries to show the first time a word was ever used and the different ways it’s been used over the years. But in Hebrew, when a word is only used once, scholars kind of go with what the traditional meaning was that they were given as students. “Well, it’s translated in the Greek as this, and they knew more Hebrew than we do, so it’s probably close to this.” It works with the names as well. 

---- 

Names are connected to experience and to memory. When I first got into ministry, people would tell me about how hard it is to remember people’s names, how they struggle with it. The names we remember are the ones that matter to us. They are the names that we think about. We don’t forget those names. We like to think of ourselves as people who are equally thoughtful to all the people in our lives, but that is not the case. 

There are tricks you can do to remember names. One trick I think is very helpful is this: when you meet somebody, use their name a lot, really quickly. “Nice to meet you, Jack. It’s great to see you, Jack. What an interesting outfit you got there, Jack. So what do you do, Jack?” You just keep repeating it over and over again, and that tends to help. But again, for people I’m never going to see again, it’s not the most important thing in the world. It’s about what matters in my life.

The names written down in Nehemiah matter to God — the builders who built the wall. 

### Emmaus Transition
In the New Testament, we have passages like the one today, the road to Emmaus, where we don’t have names for the disciples. That doesn’t mean they don’t matter to God, but there’s something else going on here.

We have a different kind of building going on in the Gospel of Luke. Instead of building a wall, they’re building a church. They’re building the people of God by walking with Jesus.

What does it take to build the people of God?

What we see here is that the nameless disciples were moved by the story of Jesus to the point that they were sharing it with the people around them — the people who had not heard, or who did not act as if they had heard. As I preached about at Easter, Jesus appeared here as a stranger. He might have appeared different for each of those nameless disciples who walked with him, who walked next to him, who didn’t recognize him because he was a stranger. And yet their eyes were opened — sharing food together, sharing fellowship together, sharing life together, sharing community together. Building something for tomorrow.

We were all strangers once to each other. Sometimes we feel like strangers to people on the other side of the room from us at church because it’s rare for anyone to sit in a different section than the one they first sat in when they came. 

And yet, if you don’t know the name of the person sitting on the other side of the sanctuary from you or if this is your first time with us today, we are not strangers here because we all walk with the same person. We are known and understood and remembered by walking with Jesus Christ. Whether our faith has been something important to us for a long time or it is a new experience, we are no longer strangers because of who God is in Jesus Christ. As Paul writes in Galatians 3:

>There is no longer Jew or Greek; there is no longer slave or free; there is no longer male and female, for all of you are one in Christ Jesus.

As we walk together, we build something greater than ourselves. As we walk together, sometimes we carry each other and sometimes we are carried. As we walk together sometimes we notice aspects of Jesus in each other and sometimes people move this aspects of Jesus in us. That is Grace. That is amazing grace. When God carried us so much up on Eagles wings that we no longer even look like the broken sad people we used to be. 

Instead, when people look at us, they see our savior. That is the power of resurrection. It is not just that we are saved, but also that we are called to participate in the salvation of the world. To build up the kingdom of heaven. To welcome people to walk next to us on this journey. To invite people to walk next to us on this journey. We do this not because we are experts, but because Christ is risen.

The names in Nehemiah matter. The nameless Saints in Luke 24 matter. There are names that God knows of people yet to walk through these doors who are going to meet Jesus here and have their lives changed. We are building something beyond even now through God.

Now friends, in a little bit we’re going to hear from a few people about the capital campaign we’re running right now, to try and take down a good chunk of our debt. Part of that is going to be telling the story of why that debt exists to begin with. A lot of us who are here came to Covenant after all those decisions were made. Some of us were here through it all. It is all of our legacy here. There are names of those who built it, but there are also our names today — making a decision and a commitment to build for the future of this church and the future of the presence of God in our community. Not being limited by the decisions of yesterday.

Because sometimes this is a way to understand what was going on with Nehemiah. When Nehemiah was rebuilding the wall, when he was called by God to rebuild the wall, he was not being trapped by the decisions of the generations before him that led to exile and the destruction of that wall. He didn’t say, “Well, it’s not my problem.” He took on that responsibility and inspired others to do so — for a purpose.

When those disciples on the road to Emmaus saw the man on the road, they did not look at him and say, “Not my responsibility.” They welcomed him, shared stories with him, walked together with him — and then discovered it was Jesus, all along.

In this season of resurrection, Jesus will continue to come and show us who he is. Revive us, again and again. May we open ourselves up to him. May we open ourselves up to the movement of the Spirit. May we open ourselves up to be builders of today for the kingdom of God tomorrow.

In the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

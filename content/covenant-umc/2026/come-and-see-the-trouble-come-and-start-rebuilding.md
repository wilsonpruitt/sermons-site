**Series:** Easter

> Preached on the Second Sunday of Easter, this sermon pairs Doubting Thomas (John 20:19-31) with Nehemiah inspecting Jerusalem's ruined walls (Nehemiah 2:11-20) to launch Covenant UMC's capital campaign to pay down roughly $358,000 of sanctuary debt. Pruitt argues that resurrection should reshape how Christians see everything—including money—and that congregations are eager for Jesus's costly self-gift on the cross but reluctant for the ongoing 'maintenance' of building the kingdom on that foundation. Using the Pruitt-Igoe housing project as a cautionary tale that the true cost of any building lies in upkeep and rebuilding, he reframes Thomas's bare-facts realism as a commendable financial diligence, names his own avoidance of money-talk as a ministry weakness, and calls the church—like Nehemiah—to look honestly at fallen walls and rebuild faithfully (not foolhardily) so resources can flow into ministry. Our scars, like the risen Christ's, are not erased by resurrection but redeemed.

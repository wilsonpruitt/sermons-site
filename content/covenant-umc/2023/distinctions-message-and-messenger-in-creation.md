**Series:** Creation

> Preaching from Genesis 1:6-13 and John 1:6-9, the sermon argues that creation proceeds by distinctions (light/dark, sky/sea, sea/land, living/non-living) and that the deepest distinction is between message and messenger: Jesus is the light and the message, while John the Baptist, the Bible, holy people, and the church are messengers who point to him. Confusing the two is idolatry, illustrated by the Golden Calf, Simon the magician, and Calvin's 'idol factory' heart; idolatry's great enemy is patience, a fruit of the Spirit. The Holy Spirit is fully God and present with us without being us (panentheism, not pantheism). The sermon turns to baptism's waters of creation and to stewardship, previewing Covenant UMC's stewardship campaign and a congregational conversation, and closes with a Dayenu meditation on God's overflowing generosity from creation through cross, resurrection, and the gift of the Spirit.

Philosophers and theologians talk about distinctions in exhausting ways that don’t seem to matter. A classic question of medieval philosophy was how many angels could dance on the head of a pin? It seems like the most useless questions imaginable, at a first glance. The issue, though, is not the amount, but whether angels are material or non-material beings. Can angels be present around us and not follow the laws of material reality, or are they just hidden and we will bump into them or do they even exist? All of these interesting thoughts come out of this seemingly absurd question about dancing angels.

Another philosophical distinction that seems empty at first is between pantheism and Panentheism. There is a one-syllable distinction that makes a big difference. There are other words like this: heartache and heart attack. You know, there's only a one syllable difference between heartache and heart attack. Yep. There is a major difference. There's a one syllable difference between pantheism and pantheism, but there's a major reality of difference. Pantheism is that God is everywhere. Trees=God, mice=God, birds=God pools, God, okay, this is pantheism. 

Panentheism is about the presence of God. God is in the trees. God is in the birds. God is present but not equivalent to creation. 
There's a distinction that matters. We don't worship creation. We worship the God who created everything. We see evidence in creation. Vestiges in creation. Vestige is this great Latin word that means footprints. You see footprints of God in creation, but God is not creation. With John the Baptist, we see vestiges of the Good News of Jesus Christ, but John the Baptist is not Jesus Christ. With holy people that we have known, we see evidence of Jesus, but they're not the same as Jesus. They aren't who we worship. They point, they testify to God and God is present with him.
In a similar way, the Bible is a messenger of Jesus, it is not Jesus. If we don’t have the messenger, we don’t have Jesus, but the messenger is not Jesus. 

This is ultimately what happened with the Golden Calf during the Exodus from Egypt. Moses was on the mountaintop and the people wanted a god to worship. They wanted an object to look at it. They didn’t care what it was. They wanted to See the message, not just hear it. So Aaron made the calf. They were impatient, so they began to worship the messenger and not the message. This is what idolatry does. It takes something good, and then twists and distorts it. In the case of the golden calf, it was the desire of the people to follow God, which is good on its own. But they didn’t want to wait for Moses to come down the mountain, so they had Aaron form his own God. 

John Calvin called the human heart an idol factory. Idolatry is not a question of worship but attention. Religious devotion is about our attention. What are we attending? Who are we attending? This attention can go in a million different directions and we can create idols like sacred cows. Good-hearted people have been deceived by charlatans again and again. 

I say that to remind us that there is no space safe from the temptation to idolatry. 

The great enemy of idolatry, however, is patience. Patience is a fruit of the Spirit for a reason. The Holy Spirit both gives us patience and encourages us to be patient by faith. We don’t need to rush to the next thing because we have been given faith. 

The Holy Spirit is with us when we cannot see it because the Holy Spirit is not a created reality. The Holy Spirit is fully God and is fully present with us. This is the power of the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit is with us, but it isn't us. It is an important distinction to make.

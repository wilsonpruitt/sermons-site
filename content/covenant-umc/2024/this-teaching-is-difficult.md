**Series:** After Pentecost

> Preaching from John 6:56-69 and Ephesians 6:10-20, Wilson argues that the teachings of Jesus are genuinely difficult and that it is honest to admit so. Opening with how protective gear (lab goggles, X-ray shielding, football helmets, military gear) always signals danger, he frames the armor of God as the protective equipment for facing the hard demands of faith: believing bread and juice are Christ's body and blood, loving enemies, and following Jesus when many disciples turn back. Surveying denominational views of communion and Wesley's Duty of Constant Communion, he affirms that difficulty doesn't mean avoidance. Peter's confession ('Lord, to whom can we go?') models faithful perseverance. The armor of God, especially the belt of truth (God's love for you), guards against the world's deceptions, and the sermon closes with the invitation that even hard discipleship finds rest in Christ's easy yoke.

## Ephesians
At the end of the letter to the Ephesians, Paul lays out fully this concept of the armor of God. He briefly touched on it in an earlier letter in the end of First Thessalonians. We recently finished that letter in my Bible study on the letters of Paul, and it was really fascinating to go through the text super slowly and intentionally and see how Paul's argument was being developed and the ending in this crescendo of sorts, this, he is getting louder and more powerful. He's going from writing to preaching, to singing words of praise. Near the very end of 1 Thessalonians, Paul describes putting on the breastplate of justice, but here in Ephesians, he lays out the different levels of the armor of God. Shield of faith. Breastplate of justice or righteousness. Belt of truth.

The passage from Ephesians is super connected to the end of John six that I read for the gospel reading because in the end of John six, Jesus is laying out his understanding of communion, of offering his body and blood to people. 
It's scandalous, it's literally scandalous. Scandal is the Greek word here. It is scandalous is offering his body and blood and if you eat his body and blood, you will have eternal life. There is a lot of grumbling going on and the disciples say, “People are saying, Jesus, this is difficult.” 
This is difficult. It's a difficult teaching. It's hard. There's a challenge to it. We may need to put some other armor on, some protective gear, in order to really face the challenge on our own. 
In this world, it is sometimes too hard to face the difficulties of the teachings of Jesus on our own. Just you, by yourself, in this world, it's hard to love your enemy as yourself. First of all, you have to admit you have enemies. This is something we don't like to do today, and I define enemies in a lot of, in broad way, but I think it's important to define it. 
The enemy is the person whom you don't want to see at HEB. The person you don't want to see at your front door. The number you don't want to pick up when they call, it may not be someone you're intentionally trying to tear down, but someone you're trying to avoid. Someone whom you feel, if you never saw them again for the rest of your life, your life would improve. It is a difficult teaching to love them, yet this is what Jesus commands us to do. 
It is a difficult teaching to see bread and juice as the body of Christ.

For Catholics, it's specifically articulated using the language the philosophical language of Aristotle, and so they talk about substance and accidents. So Catholics think that it's all Jesus. Lutherans have this under kind of understanding that the bread is both Jesus and bread, Calvinists, reformed Presbyterians have a different understanding of communion. 
That has to do with the presence of Jesus at the table and in the world.

Just because things are difficult though doesn't mean that they're not worth doing. In fact,

A challenge gives us purpose and demands some kind of overcoming. A life without challenge is a life of lies. 
This is where the armor of God comes in. The armor of God is there for us to face the challenges of this world and the challenges of this faith, fully remembering that we are not alone, and that despite it being difficult, it is not impossible because we have been given these tools to face the challenges of faith and of this world.
